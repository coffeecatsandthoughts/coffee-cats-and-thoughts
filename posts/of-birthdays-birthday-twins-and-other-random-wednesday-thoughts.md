I'm currently reading a book about a woman who becomes obsessed with her birthday twin and starts doing some pretty creepy things. And because my brain apparently cannot just read a book without wandering off into completely unrelated territory, it got me thinking about the people I've shared my birthday with.

For the record, I'm nowhere near becoming obsessed with anyone who happens to have been born on the same day as me. I have two kids, a husband, cats, a household to run, and bills to pay. I barely have time to obsess over my own life, much less somebody else's. 

Anyway, in my four decades and counting, I've known five people who share my birthday. There's Tita Jonee, our neighbor in Baguio; Tita Delia, my good friend Janice's mother; Nicole, a friend from college; and my niece Alexa and nephew Chance. Five people whose birthdays I've never had much trouble remembering because, well, they're also mine. Convenient, really.

Yesterday, though, I learned that Tita Delia had unexpectedly passed away. And just like that, the number of people I know who share my birthday went from five to four.

It's a strange thing to think about. Every year, on the same date, we celebrate another trip around the sun. We blow out candles, receive greetings, and perhaps complain about getting older. And somewhere along the way, someone who has been marking that same day alongside us is no longer here to celebrate it.

Of course, sharing a birthday doesn't necessarily mean sharing anything else. We have different lives, different experiences, and different stories. The date is just a coincidence. Still, there's something oddly comforting about knowing that someone you know is celebrating another year of life on the exact same day you are.

And then, because apparently that wasn't enough thinking for one Wednesday, my mind wandered even further.

What about someone who was born on the exact same day AND year as you? In the same city? Maybe even in the same hospital? Imagine two babies born just a few hours apart, perhaps in neighboring rooms, whose mothers might have crossed paths in the hallway without ever knowing that their children would grow up sharing that tiny piece of history.

This is especially interesting to me because I was born at Dr. Jose Fabella Memorial Hospital in Manila, a maternity hospital that was already handling an astonishing number of deliveries decades ago. Historical records suggest it was averaging around 80 to 90 deliveries a day in the late 1960s and mid-1980s. I don't know exactly how many babies were born there on my birthday, but considering those numbers, there could have been dozens of us who entered the world in the same hospital, on the very same day.

So where are these people now? Are they married? Do they have children? Are they also in their 40s, wondering why their backs hurt after sleeping in a perfectly normal position? 

Seriously, though. Somewhere out there could be an entire group of people who share my birthday, birth year, and birthplace. Some of us might even have been delivered in the same room, just hours apart. And none of us has the slightest idea the others exist.

What are the odds that we'd ever meet? Or that we already have, without realizing it?

Maybe I've stood behind one of them in a grocery store. Maybe we've sat beside each other on a bus, worked in the same building, or exchanged a polite smile while waiting in line for coffee. We could have spent an entire afternoon in the same room without knowing that our lives began on the very same date, in the very same hospital, possibly just a few hours apart.

And what would we even do if we found out?

Personally, I'd probably say, "Oh, really? Me too!" Then we'd compare birth years, laugh about the coincidence, and go back to whatever we were doing. Because unlike the woman in my book, I have absolutely no intention of making somebody else's birthday my entire personality. 

Still, I can't help wondering how many people out there share more of our beginnings than we'll ever know. It's funny how we can go through life feeling that our experiences are entirely our own, only to realize that a complete stranger might share a small but oddly specific piece of our story.

And somewhere in the middle of all this unnecessary curiosity, I find myself thinking about Tita Delia again.

I suppose yesterday's news reminded me that while birthdays are usually about counting the years we've been given, they're also a reminder that not everyone gets another one. I've always associated her with my birthday, and I probably always will. Only now, when that date comes around, I'll remember that someone who used to celebrate it with me is no longer here.

It's a sad thought, especially because her passing was so unexpected. And perhaps that's why this whole birthday coincidence has been on my mind more than it ordinarily would.

As for the rest of this little existential detour, well, I've been awake for 17 hours. I'm supposed to be sleeping, but instead I'm here wondering whether someone born in the same hospital as me, on the same day, is out there somewhere wondering the same thing.

Or maybe they're just going about their day, completely unaware that some random woman who shares their birthday is currently contemplating their existence.

Meanwhile, I'm losing sleep over people I don't even know exist.
