<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The surgery is over. The fibroid is gone. The cyst is out. By all accounts, I should probably be feeling relieved. And I am... mostly. But if there's one thing I've learned about being an overthinker, it's that the finish line has a way of moving.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Now my mind has found something else to obsess over.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The biopsy. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The rational side of me knows it's standard procedure. They remove something, they send it to the lab, they check it under a microscope. It doesn't automatically mean they found something sinister. But anxiety isn't exactly known for listening to reason. It has a habit of filling in blank spaces with worst-case scenarios.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The other day, while those thoughts were making themselves comfortable in my head, I happened to glance at the time. It was 10:52 AM. On a whim, I searched for the Bible verse with the same numbers.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:image {"id":595,"sizeSlug":"large"} -->
<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img src="https://coffeecatsandthoughts.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image_editor_output_image-1658949658-17854642148388677507403892352628.jpg?w=844" alt="" class="wp-image-595" /></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>"Go," said Jesus, "your faith has healed you."</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I just stared at it for a while. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Did I suddenly know what the biopsy results would be? No. The uncertainty didn't magically disappear, and neither did the waiting. But something else did.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The noise. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Because maybe that's what I needed more than answers in that moment. Not certainty about tomorrow, but peace for today. A reminder that I don't have to carry every "what if" before it even exists.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I realized that overthinking is really just my way of trying to control what I can't. I convince myself that if I worry enough, I'll somehow be more prepared for bad news. But all it really does is make me suffer twice. Once in my imagination, and maybe never in reality.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That day, the overthinker in me was gently interrupted.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Not by a medical report. Not by reassurance from Google. But by a simple verse that reminded me where my focus should be. Whatever the biopsy says will be what it is. Worrying about it today won't change tomorrow's results.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>So for now, I'll do the hardest thing an overthinker can do. I'll wait. And I'll trust.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Because once again, my anxious mind had plenty to say. And once again, the Word of the Lord had the final word. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
