<!-- wp:paragraph -->

<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The answer didn't scare me.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In a strange way, it was a relief. The endless guessing, the what-ifs, the wondering whether it was something worse. All of that quieted down a little.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What lingers now isn't fear of the diagnosis.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It's the realization that there are decisions to make. Risks to consider. Consent forms to sign. Conversations about surgery that suddenly don't feel hypothetical anymore.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I know doctors do these procedures every day. I know the odds are generally good.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>But it's hard to take comfort in statistics when you're sitting there thinking about having thalassemia, enlarged heart, being bradycardic, and wondering whether you'll still be one of the people those statistics work out for.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Maybe that's what I'm struggling with. Not about what was found, but the fact that being told "we found it" is only the beginning of what comes next.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Or I'm probably overdramatic. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
