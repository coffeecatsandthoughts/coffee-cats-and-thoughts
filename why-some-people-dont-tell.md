<p></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Three years ago, my cousin told me a secret. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Her cancer had come back. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>She didn't tell anyone. Not even her parents. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>At the time, I couldn't understand it. I remember carrying that knowledge with me and wondering how she managed to carry it herself. Part of me wanted to tell her to let people in, to allow others to share the burden. Looking back, it felt like a lonely decision.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Lately, though, I've found myself thinking about her more often.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Not because our situations are the same. They're not. But because for the first time, I think I understand a little bit of what she may have felr.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Over the past few weeks, I've realized that there is a difference between carrying a worry and sharing it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Most people assume that telling someone makes things easier. Sometimes it does.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>But sometimes sharing a worry means watching it spread. The moment you tell someone who loves you, the concern stops belonging only to you. It becomes theirs too.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>And that's where things get complicated. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Because there are moments when I want to tell people exactly what I'm thinking. There are moments when I want to say, "I'm worried." Not because I need answers, but because sometimes saying things out loud makes them feel less heavy.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Then I remember what happens when you share a worry with someone who loves you.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>They start carrying it too. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>They check on you more often. They ask questions you don't yet have answers to. They worry in the quiet moments. They imagine possibilities you'd rather not think about yet. And before long, the thing you were trying to process for yourself becomes something other people are carrying alongside you.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>That's not a bad thing. In fact, it's probably one of the greatest expressions of love.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>But, it is a burden. And, I think that's the part I never fully understood before. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Maybe when my cousin kept her illness to herself, it wasn't because she didn't trust the people around her. Maybe it wasn't even because she wanted to be strong.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Maybe she simply knew how heavy that knowledge could be once it was shared.Maybe she was protecting the people she loved from carrying it before they had to.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I don't know if that's true. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I only know that lately, I've caught myself doing the same thing. Starting conversations in my head. Imagining who I should tell and what I would say. Then deciding not to.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For now, these thoughts will probably stay here.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Not because they're a secret.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Just because this little corner of the internet, on a blog that almost nobody reads, feels like a safe place to set them down for a while.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
