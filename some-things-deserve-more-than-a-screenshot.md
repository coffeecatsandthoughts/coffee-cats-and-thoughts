<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Maybe it's just me, but people seem to be in a hurry to judge these days.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Not just in one particular situation, but in general.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We live in a time where information travels faster than ever. Within minutes, screenshots, posts, videos, opinions, and reactions can reach thousands of people. Yet for all the information available to us, understanding seems to be in short supply.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>What surprises me is how quickly people feel compelled to form an opinion. We barely know the facts, yet somehow we've already chosen sides, assigned motives, and determined who deserves our sympathy and who deserves our condemnation.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>It's tiring. </p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Not because people shouldn't care. They should. Not because serious issues shouldn't be taken seriously. They absolutely should. But somewhere along the way, we've become uncomfortable with admitting that we don't know everything.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>"We don't know enough yet."</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>"I'd like to hear more before I say anything."</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>"There may be details I don't know."</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Those used to be perfectly acceptable responses. Now it feels as though uncertainty itself has become unpopular.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Maybe that's what bothers me most. Not the discussions themselves, but the certainty.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The confidence with which people mock or celebrate another person's downfall based on fragments of information they saw online. Sometimes from people who know very little about the situation. Sometimes from people who know very little about the person. And sometimes from people who, if we're being honest, don't even behave particularly well in their own lives.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Discernment isn't the same as indifference. Compassion isn't the same as approval. And choosing to pause before passing judgment doesn't mean you're excusing wrongdoing.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Sometimes it simply means you're humble enough to admit that truth deserves more than a few seconds of scrolling before you decide what to believe.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I don't know. Maybe in a world where everyone feels pressured to react immediately, choosing to pause has become a lost skill.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
